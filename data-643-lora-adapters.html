8.3 LoRA, Adapters, and Federated Updates

DATA 443/643 · Week 8 · PDF · Slides

A low-rank pair BA instead of rewriting W. Small deltas you can ship or keep local.

These notes match the lecture slides. Use (slides) on the course hub for the deck.

Full fine-tuning moves every entry of W. LoRA (Hu et al., 2021) freezes W and learns a low-rank residual. You store a few megabytes per task instead of a second copy of the 7B.

1. W+BA

For a linear map W∈ℝd×k,

h=Wx+αrBAx,

with A∈ℝr×k, B∈ℝd×r, rank r≪min(d,k). Initialize B=0 so the adapter starts as the base model. Train only A,B (and maybe a bias). Attention projections and MLP up/down maps are the usual insertion points.

QLoRA quantizes W to 4-bit and still trains float adapters. Same algebra. Lab 8 uses a 4×4 frozen W so you can print BA.

2. Rank, merge, many tasks

Small r (4, 8, 16) is often enough for style and instruction shifts; harder domain shifts may want larger r or more layers. At deploy time you can merge W←W+(α/r)BA and throw the adapter away, or keep several adapters and swap them.

One adapter per skill is how you avoid the XOR-then-AND overwrite (note 8.2): the base stays, the skill is a file.

3. Federated adapters

Sites that cannot ship raw text can still train a LoRA locally and send A,B to a server. The server averages adapters (or stacks them) without seeing documents. That is federated fine-tuning in the sense this course needs: communication is the adapter, not the corpus. Privacy is not automatic (adapters can leak), but the footprint is the right size.

For a project: report r, which modules you adapted, α, and whether you merged. Compare to a small full fine-tune if you can afford it.

4. Practice

  1. If r=1 and d=k=4, how many trainable numbers are in (A,B) versus W?

  2. Why initialize B=0 rather than A=0?

  3. A hospital trains a LoRA and sends it to you. What did they not send, and what can still leak?
