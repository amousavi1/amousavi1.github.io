9.3 Direct Preference Optimization

DATA 443/643 · Week 9 · PDF · Slides

Same pairs. Implicit r = β log(π/π_ref). Z(x) cancels. Offline.

These notes match the lecture slides. Use (slides) on the course hub for the deck.

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) (Rafailov et al., 2023) fits a policy to chosen/rejected pairs without training an RM and without PPO. The reward is written in closed form from the policy and a frozen reference, then the Bradley–Terry model becomes a classification loss on logits.

1. Skip the reward model

RLHF solves maxπ𝔼[r]−βKL(π‖πref). Under Bradley–Terry, the optimal r can be expressed as

r(x,y)=βlogπ(y∣x)πref(y∣x)+const(x).

Plug that into the RM logistic loss and the r parameters disappear. You train πθ directly.

Stanford CS224N 2025 L10 writes the same derived reward and stresses that the partition Z(x) cancels because Bradley–Terry only sees a difference. KTO and IPO sit in the same family; this hour is DPO.

2. The loss

ℒDPO=−logσ(βlogπθ(yw∣x)πref(yw∣x)−βlogπθ(yl∣x)πref(yl∣x)).

In a batch you need log-probabilities of both completions under πθ and under πref. The reference stays frozen (usually SFT). β is the same kind of leash as in RLHF: it scales how hard you push away from πref.

Lab 9 will compute this on toy scalar logits, not on an LLM.

If πθ=πref, both log-ratios are 0, the argument of σ is 0, and σ(0)=1/2. Then ℒDPO=log2≈0.693. That is the start of training, not a bug.

Write the argument of σ as β(Δlogπθ−Δlogπref) if it helps: you want πθ to put relatively more mass on yw than the reference did, compared with yl. Absolute log-prob can fall on both sequences; relative log-prob is what DPO scores.

3. When DPO is the right picture

You have pairs, a GPU, and no appetite for a PPO stack. Offline: the pair set is fixed, so you do not explore new y unless you collect more. If the SFT model never proposed good yw, DPO cannot invent them from nothing. Implicit reward can still overfit the pair set.

Report β, the reference checkpoint, and a held-out preference accuracy (how often πθ assigns higher log-prob to yw than to yl). That number is not “the model is aligned”; it is “the model copied this rater.”

4. Teaching this note

About 35–40 minutes at the board: write implicit r, plug into σ, compute σ(0)=1/2. Play the Deep Dive preference / RLHF segment 2:48:26–3:09:39. Lab 9 section 2 is the toy DPO scalar.

5. Worked example

Let β=1. Suppose at init πθ=πref. Then

logπθ(yw)πref(yw)−logπθ(yl)πref(yl)=0,

so ℒ=−logσ(0)=−log(1/2)=log2.

After a few steps, toy log-probs (sums over tokens): logπθ(yw)=−2.0, logπref(yw)=−2.5, logπθ(yl)=−3.0, logπref(yl)=−2.0. Log-ratio winner: −2.0−(−2.5)=0.5. Log-ratio loser: −3.0−(−2.0)=−1.0. Difference 0.5−(−1.0)=1.5. σ(1.5)≈0.818, loss −log0.818≈0.201.

The frozen reference is still there: without it, πθ can raise both log-probs or collapse. PPO can sample new y that were not in the pair file; offline DPO cannot.

6. Where students get stuck

  • Forgetting σ(0)=1/2 when πθ=πref at step 0.
  • Dropping πref because “there is no RM.” The reference is the leash.
  • Expecting DPO to invent replies the SFT model never wrote.

8. Practice

  1. If πθ=πref, what is ℒDPO? (Compute σ(0).)

  2. Why do you still keep a frozen reference if there is no RM?

  3. Name one thing PPO can do that offline DPO cannot, given a fixed pair file.

  4. β=2, winner log-ratio 0.4, loser log-ratio 0.1. What is the argument of σ, and is the loss smaller or larger than log2?

  5. Both completions have log-prob −10 under πθ and under πref. What is the DPO loss, and what did the policy fail to learn yet?
