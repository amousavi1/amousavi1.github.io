1.2 Why Vector Calculus

DATA 442/642 · Week 1 · PDF

Training is an optimization problem. Linear regression is the first example.

1. Training is optimization

A model is a function of parameters θ. A cost J(θ) says how well those parameters explain the data. Training is

θ^∈\argminθJ(θ).

Three pictures you will see again:

  • Linear regression. θ are weights. J is squared error (or a likelihood).
  • Auto-encoders (Module 6). θ are layer weights. J is reconstruction error. The chain rule is how you differentiate through the stack.
  • Gaussian mixtures (Module 5). θ are means, covariances, and mixture weights. J is a (negative) likelihood.

If you cannot write J and say what θ is, you do not yet have a learning problem.

2. Linear regression, written so the calculus is visible

Training pairs (yn,𝐱n), n=1,…,N. Prediction y^n=θ^⊤𝐱n. Cost

J(θ)=∑n=1N(yn−θ⊤𝐱n)2.

Set ∇J=0. In matrix form that is the normal equation

(𝐗⊤𝐗)θ^=𝐗⊤𝐲,θ^=(𝐗⊤𝐗)−1𝐗⊤𝐲

when 𝐗⊤𝐗 is invertible. The inverse existing is a linear-algebra fact (full column rank). The ∇J=0 step is calculus.

For two weights the surface J is a convex bowl. One local minimum, and it is global. Neural nets will not be this kind.

3. What Module 1 still owes you

To minimize a smooth J you need:

  • a gradient (first derivatives) — when to stop, which way is downhill;
  • a Hessian (second derivatives) — how curved the bowl is;
  • later, constraints (week 3) when θ is not free in ℝl.

The next two notes are those derivatives. Week 2 turns them into algorithms.
