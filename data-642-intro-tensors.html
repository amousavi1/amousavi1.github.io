8.1 Introduction to Tensors

DATA 442/642 · Week 8 · PDF · Slides

Multi-way arrays and why flattening loses structure.

These notes walk through the lecture slides. The original deck is unchanged; use (slides) on the course hub if you want that PDF.

Figures and notes follow Introduction to Tensor Decompositions and their Applications in Machine Learning.

1. Brief history

Tensors generalize matrices to higher dimensions: multidimensional arrays.

They and their decompositions appear in 1927, then sit unused in computer science until late in the 20th century. Cheaper compute and better multilinear algebra — especially in the last decade — moved them into statistics, data science, and machine learning.

2. Tensor basics

A tensor is a multi-way collection of numbers, usually from ℝ. The simplest high-dimensional case is a three-dimensional array: a data cube. The same notation extends to more modes.

3. Tensor order

The order of a tensor is the number of dimensions.

Object Order Notation
Scalar 0 x∈ℝ
Vector 1 𝐱∈ℝI1
Matrix 2 𝐗∈ℝI1×I2
Higher-order tensor N 𝒳∈ℝI1×I2×⋯×IN

Each I is the length of that mode.

4. Tensor indexing

Fix some indices to get a subarray.

  • Fibers: fix every index but one.
  • Slices (slabs): fix every index but two.

For a third-order tensor, fibers are x:jk=xjk (column), xi:k (row), xij: (tube). Slices are 𝒳::k=𝒳k (frontal), 𝒳:j: (lateral), 𝒳i:: (horizontal).

5. Outer and inner product

The vector outer product is the product of the entries, written ⊚. For two length-n vectors 𝐚 and 𝐛,

𝐗=𝐚⊚𝐛=𝐚𝐛⊤.

For N vectors the same product is a tensor:

𝒳=𝐚(1)⊚𝐚(2)⊚⋯⊚𝐚(N),

with xi1i2⋯iN=ai1(1)ai2(2)⋯aiN(N).

The inner product of two length-n vectors is a scalar:

x=⟨𝐚,𝐛⟩=𝐚⊤𝐛=∑i=1naibi.

6. Rank-1 tensors

An N-way tensor is rank-1 if it is exactly the outer product of N vectors. Adding a mode is a new scaling of a sub-tensor.

A rank-1 matrix: 𝐗=𝐚⊚𝐛. A rank-1 3-way tensor: 𝒳=𝐚⊚𝐛⊚𝐜. The N-way form is the product above.

7. Tensor rank

The rank rank(𝒳)=R is the smallest number of rank-1 tensors that sum to 𝒳:

𝒳=∑r=1Rλr𝐚r(1)⊚𝐚r(2)⊚⋯⊚𝐚r(N).

The factor matrices 𝐀 store those vectors as columns:

𝐀=[𝐚1𝐚2⋯𝐚R].

The weights λr (with λ∈ℝR) often absorb column norms, typically so each column has squared length one.
