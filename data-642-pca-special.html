6.3 Special Considerations for PCA

DATA 442/642 · Week 6 · PDF · Slides

Centering, scaling, and how many components to keep.

These notes walk through the lecture slides. The original deck is unchanged; use (slides) on the course hub if you want that PDF.

Figures and notes follow Deisenroth, Faisal, and Ong, Mathematics for Machine Learning; Theodoridis; and Géron.

1. PCA and singular value decomposition (SVD)

Eigendecomposition. From the covariance 𝐒=1N∑n=1Nxnxn⊤, compute eigenvalues λi and eigenvectors ui. The ui are the principal components. Each λi is the variance along that component.

SVD. Factor the centered data matrix: 𝐗=𝐔Σ𝐕⊤. The right singular vectors 𝐕 are the principal components. The singular values on the diagonal of Σ are the square roots of the eigenvalues of 𝐗⊤𝐗.

The two routes agree. That is the link between the maximum-variance view of PCA and the SVD of 𝐗.

2. PCA and low-rank approximations

Because 𝐗^ is the best rank-m approximation of 𝐗 in the Frobenius sense,

‖𝐗^−𝐗‖F2=∑n=1N‖x^n−xn‖2

is minimum.

So PCA also minimizes the sum of squared reconstruction errors. The same components that maximize variance are the ones that keep as much of the original points as an m-dimensional picture can.

3. Latent variable perspective

So far PCA had no probabilistic model: maximum variance and projection only. A probabilistic model would:

  • come with a likelihood, so noise in the observations is explicit;
  • allow Bayesian model comparison via the marginal likelihood;
  • treat PCA as a generative model (simulate new data);
  • make connections to related algorithms straightforward;
  • handle dimensions missing at random by Bayes;
  • give a notion of how novel a new point is;
  • extend cleanly, e.g. to a mixture of PCA models;
  • recover the PCA of the earlier sections as a special case;
  • allow a fully Bayesian treatment by integrating out parameters.

4. History of probabilistic PCA

A continuous latent z∈ℝM lets you write PCA as a probabilistic latent-variable model. Tipping and Bishop (1999) called this probabilistic PCA (PPCA). It addresses most of the list above. Ordinary PCA — maximize projected variance, or minimize reconstruction error — is maximum likelihood in the noise-free limit.

5. The PPCA model

Assume a continuous latent z∈ℝM with standard-normal prior p(z)=𝒩(0,𝐈), and a linear map to the observation x:

x=Bz+μ+ϵ∈ℝD,

where ϵ~𝒩(0,σ2𝐈) is Gaussian observation noise, B∈ℝD×M, and μ∈ℝD. The conditional is

p(x∣z;B,μ,σ2)=𝒩(x∣Bz+μ,σ2𝐈).

The generative process:

zn~𝒩(z∣0,𝐈)

and

xn∣zn~𝒩(x∣Bzn+μ,σ2𝐈).

To draw a typical point, use ancestral sampling: first zn~p(z), then xn~p(x∣zn;B,μ,σ2).

The joint is

p(x,z∣B,μ,σ2)=p(x∣z,B,μ,σ2)p(z).

6. An illustration of PPCA

Blue dots: two-dimensional PCA latents for MNIST digits “8”. Query any z* in that plane and generate x~*=Bz* (here μ=0 and identity covariance). The image looks like an “8”.

  2. Besides maximizing variance, what reconstruction quantity does a rank-m PCA minimize?

  3. In PPCA, what extra random object appears that ordinary PCA never named, and what prior does it get?
