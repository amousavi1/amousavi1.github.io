12.2 The Reverse Process

DATA 443/643 · Week 12 · PDF · Slides

Learn to denoise. Each step predicts the noise (or the clean sample) and takes a step back.

These notes match the lecture slides. Use (slides) on the course hub for the deck.

Generation walks the forward process backward. You start from xT~𝒩(0,𝐈) and, step by step, remove a bit of noise until you have x0.

1. Predict the noise, not the image

Ho et al. (DDPM) train a net εθ(xt,t) to guess the ε you added in note 12.1. The usual loss is

𝔼t,x0,ε\|ε−εθ(xt,t)\|2.

Predicting ε is equivalent (up to scaling) to predicting x0 or the score ∇xtlogq(xt). Pick one parameterization and stay consistent.

2. One reverse step

Given xt and a noise prediction, a typical mean for xt−1 is

xt−1=1αt(xt−1−αt1−α¯tεθ(xt,t))+σtz,

with z~𝒩(0,𝐈) for t>1, and σt from the schedule (often σt2=βt).

If you knew x0, the true posterior q(xt−1∣xt,x0) is Gaussian with a closed-form mean. Lab 12 uses that oracle mean for a single step so you can see denoising without training a U-Net.

3. Sampling cost

You pay T network calls (or fewer with DDIM / distilled samplers). That is the trade: stable regression training, slow ancestral sampling. GANs (Week 11) sample in one forward pass and fight mode collapse instead.

For a project, report the sampler (T, DDPM vs DDIM) next to the metric. Changing T at test time is part of the method.

4. Practice

  1. If εθ=ε exactly, what should happen to ‖xt−1−x0‖ relative to ‖xt−x0‖, in expectation, for mid-range t?

  2. Why add σtz on the way down instead of always taking the mean?

  3. You may not train a score network in Lab 12. What replaces εθ for that one reverse step?
