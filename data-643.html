DATA 443/643: Advanced Concepts in Large Language Models

American University

Complementary notes, slides, and labs. Canvas is official for due dates and submissions. The listed prerequisite is DATA 427/627. DATA 441/641 and DATA 442/642 are not required; overlap with those courses is intentional so this sequence is self-contained. The semester is project-based. Questions: mousavi@american.edu.

Weekly Materials

Week 1 is posted. Later weeks show the syllabus map until their notes are ready. Canvas remains the official list of required reading.

Module 1 — Neural networks and word embeddings

Notes

  • 1.1 Course Map and the Semester Project(slides)
  • 1.2 Neurons, Activations, and Feedforward Nets(slides)
  • 1.3 Gradient Descent(slides)
  • 1.4 Word Embeddings and Semantic Geometry(slides)

Labs

  • Lab 1: XOR, Autograd, and a Tiny Embedding Space(PDF)

Additional readings

  • Goodfellow, Bengio, Courville: Deep feedforward networks (Ch. 6)
  • Mikolov et al., Efficient estimation of word representations
  • Jurafsky & Martin, Vector semantics and embeddings

Discussion

  • Intrinsic vs. extrinsic evaluation of embeddings: which number belongs in a project report, and which one is for debugging?
  • Name one way embedding geometry can encode a stereotype. What would you measure?
  • Why can this course skip DATA 641 and 642 and still start from neurons this week?

Module 1 — Sequence models: RNNs, LSTMs, and GRUs

Sequential modeling, vanishing and exploding gradients, gating, hidden-state dynamics.

Discussion

  • What does the hidden state have to carry across a long sentence?
  • Why do we move from recurrence to attention in Week 3?

Module 1 — Attention and transformers

Self-attention (Q/K/V), positional encoding, encoder–decoder, GPT and BERT as systems.

Discussion

  • What does a query attend to that an RNN state cannot see in one step?

Module 2 — Multimodal foundations and vision transformers

Image patches as tokens, contrastive learning, modality alignment, zero-shot transfer.

Module 2 — Vision–language models (CLIP and BLIP)

Cross-modal retrieval, captioning, bootstrapped pretraining, bias and robustness.

Module 2 — Audio and cross-modal integration

Spectrograms as tokens, fusion, unified embeddings, data scarcity.

Module 3 — Scaling, efficiency, and deployment

Scaling laws, mixture-of-experts, pruning, quantization, distillation, speculative decoding.

Module 3 — SFT, continual learning, and adapters

Instruction tuning, prompt design, catastrophic forgetting, LoRA, federated updates.

Module 3 — RLHF and direct preference optimization

Reward models, policy optimization, preference learning, human feedback.

Module 3 — Safety, editing, and retrieval-augmented training

Red-teaming, unlearning, ROME/MEMIT, RAFT, memory-augmented models.

Module 4 — Generative models I: GANs

Adversarial training, generator–discriminator dynamics, mode collapse.

Module 4 — Generative models II: diffusion

Forward and reverse processes, latent diffusion, text-to-image conditioning.

Module 4 — Reasoning and chain-of-thought

Step-by-step reasoning, self-consistency, Tree-of-Thoughts, faithfulness.

Module 4 — Tools, RAG, and final presentations

RAG pipelines, ReAct, uncertainty, evaluation of applications. Project talks.

Final exam and remaining talks

Cumulative exam. Remaining presentations as posted on Canvas.
