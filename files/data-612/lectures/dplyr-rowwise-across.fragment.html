rowwise() summarizes across columns in one row. across() repeats a function on many columns.

1. mutate() works on whole columns

Before rowwise(), look at what mutate() normally does.

exams <- tibble(
  student = c("A", "B", "C"),
  exam1 = c(80, 70, 90),
  exam2 = c(90, 85, 95),
  exam3 = c(70, 75, 85)
)

exams |> mutate(difference = exam1 - exam2, total = exam1 + exam2 + exam3)
## # A tibble: 3 × 6
##   student exam1 exam2 exam3 difference total
##   <chr>   <dbl> <dbl> <dbl>      <dbl> <dbl>
## 1 A          80    90    70        -10   240
## 2 B          70    85    75        -15   230
## 3 C          90    95    85         -5   270

It looks as if mutate() subtracts row by row. It does not. Inside mutate(), exam1 and exam2 are whole columns, so R computes

c(80, 70, 90) - c(90, 85, 95)
## [1] -10 -15  -5

- and + are vectorized: they already work element by element. No rowwise() is needed.

2. Why mean() needs rowwise()

Now try the average score of each student:

exams |> mutate(avg = mean(c(exam1, exam2, exam3)))
## # A tibble: 3 × 5
##   student exam1 exam2 exam3   avg
##   <chr>   <dbl> <dbl> <dbl> <dbl>
## 1 A          80    90    70  82.2
## 2 B          70    85    75  82.2
## 3 C          90    95    85  82.2

Every row gets the same number. c(exam1, exam2, exam3) glues the three whole columns into one vector of nine values,

c(exams$exam1, exams$exam2, exams$exam3)
## [1] 80 70 90 90 85 95 70 75 85

and mean() returns one mean of all nine, repeated on every row.

rowwise() changes the context: the next verbs run once per row.

exams |>
  rowwise() |>
  mutate(avg = mean(c(exam1, exam2, exam3))) |>
  ungroup()
## # A tibble: 3 × 5
##   student exam1 exam2 exam3   avg
##   <chr>   <dbl> <dbl> <dbl> <dbl>
## 1 A          80    90    70  80  
## 2 B          70    85    75  76.7
## 3 C          90    95    85  90

Row 1 is mean(c(80, 90, 70)) = 80, row 2 is mean(c(70, 85, 75)) = 76.67, row 3 is mean(c(90, 95, 85)) = 90.

rowwise() is a group of one

group_by(carrier) makes one group per carrier, each with many rows. rowwise() makes each row its own group. So:

  • group_by(): the context is a group of rows.
  • rowwise(): the context is one row.

rowwise(name) also keeps name as an identifier when you summarize():

df <- tibble(name = c("Mara", "Hadley"), x = 1:2, y = 3:4, z = 5:6)

df |> mutate(m = mean(c(x, y, z)))
## # A tibble: 2 × 5
##   name       x     y     z     m
##   <chr>  <int> <int> <int> <dbl>
## 1 Mara       1     3     5   3.5
## 2 Hadley     2     4     6   3.5
# one mean for the whole table

df |>
  rowwise(name) |>
  summarize(m = mean(c(x, y, z)), .groups = "drop")
## # A tibble: 2 × 2
##   name       m
##   <chr>  <dbl>
## 1 Mara       3
## 2 Hadley     4
# one mean per row

Finish with ungroup() (or .groups = "drop"). Otherwise later verbs keep working one row at a time.

3. c_across(): the values of the current row

Typing c(exam1, exam2, exam3) gets long. Inside rowwise(), c_across(exam1:exam3) collects the values of those columns in the current row into a vector. For student A it is c(80, 90, 70).

exams |>
  rowwise() |>
  mutate(
    avg_score = mean(c_across(exam1:exam3)),
    max_score = max(c_across(exam1:exam3)),
    min_score = min(c_across(exam1:exam3)),
    sd_score = sd(c_across(exam1:exam3))
  ) |>
  ungroup()
## # A tibble: 3 × 8
##   student exam1 exam2 exam3 avg_score max_score min_score sd_score
##   <chr>   <dbl> <dbl> <dbl>     <dbl>     <dbl>     <dbl>    <dbl>
## 1 A          80    90    70      80          90        70    10   
## 2 B          70    85    75      76.7        85        70     7.64
## 3 C          90    95    85      90          95        85     5

c_across() selects columns with the same helpers as select():

df <- tibble(id = 1:6, w = 10:15, x = 20:25, y = 30:35, z = 40:45)
rf <- df |> rowwise(id)

rf |> mutate(total = sum(c_across(w:z)))
## # A tibble: 6 × 6
## # Rowwise:  id
##      id     w     x     y     z total
##   <int> <int> <int> <int> <int> <int>
## 1     1    10    20    30    40   100
## 2     2    11    21    31    41   104
## 3     3    12    22    32    42   108
## 4     4    13    23    33    43   112
## 5     5    14    24    34    44   116
## 6     6    15    25    35    45   120
rf |> mutate(total = sum(c_across(where(is.numeric))))
## # A tibble: 6 × 6
## # Rowwise:  id
##      id     w     x     y     z total
##   <int> <int> <int> <int> <int> <int>
## 1     1    10    20    30    40   100
## 2     2    11    21    31    41   104
## 3     3    12    22    32    42   108
## 4     4    13    23    33    43   112
## 5     5    14    24    34    44   116
## 6     6    15    25    35    45   120

Missing values

na.rm = TRUE works as usual. It drops the NAs in that row only.

quizzes <- tibble(
  student = c("A", "B", "C"),
  quiz1 = c(80, 90, NA),
  quiz2 = c(70, NA, 85),
  quiz3 = c(90, 80, 95)
)

quizzes |>
  rowwise() |>
  mutate(quiz_avg = mean(c_across(quiz1:quiz3), na.rm = TRUE)) |>
  ungroup()
## # A tibble: 3 × 5
##   student quiz1 quiz2 quiz3 quiz_avg
##   <chr>   <dbl> <dbl> <dbl>    <dbl>
## 1 A          80    70    90       80
## 2 B          90    NA    80       85
## 3 C          NA    85    95       90

For B this is mean(c(90, NA, 80), na.rm = TRUE) = 85.

For speed on a whole frame, rowSums() and rowMeans() avoid the split-and-join:

df |> mutate(total = rowSums(across(where(is.numeric))))
## # A tibble: 6 × 6
##      id     w     x     y     z total
##   <int> <int> <int> <int> <int> <dbl>
## 1     1    10    20    30    40   101
## 2     2    11    21    31    41   106
## 3     3    12    22    32    42   111
## 4     4    13    23    33    43   116
## 5     5    14    24    34    44   121
## 6     6    15    25    35    45   126

starwars (in dplyr) is the usual practice table. Without rowwise(), max(height, mass, birth_year) is one number for the whole column stack, repeated on every row.

4. across() repeats a function on columns

across() lives inside mutate(), summarize(), filter(), and the other data-masking verbs. It does not change the data by itself.

Two arguments:

  • .cols: tidyselect, default everything()
  • .fns: a function, a named list of functions, or a formula ~ median(.x, na.rm = TRUE). .x is the current column.
starwars |>
  summarize(across(where(is.numeric), ~ median(.x, na.rm = TRUE)), .groups = "drop")
## # A tibble: 1 × 3
##   height  mass birth_year
##    <int> <dbl>      <dbl>
## 1    180    79         52
starwars |>
  group_by(species) |>
  filter(n() > 1) |>
  summarize(
    across(c(sex, gender, homeworld), ~ length(unique(.x))),
    n = n()
  )
## # A tibble: 9 × 5
##   species    sex gender homeworld     n
##   <chr>    <int>  <int>     <int> <int>
## 1 Droid        1      2         3     6
## 2 Gungan       1      1         1     3
## 3 Human        2      2        15    35
## 4 Kaminoan     2      2         1     2
## 5 Mirialan     1      1         1     2
## 6 Twi'lek      2      2         1     2
## 7 Wookiee      1      1         1     2
## 8 Zabrak       1      1         2     2
## 9 <NA>         1      1         3     4

Grouping variables are skipped so you do not summarize them by accident.

A named list makes several summaries per column:

min_max <- list(
  min = ~ min(.x, na.rm = TRUE),
  max = ~ max(.x, na.rm = TRUE)
)
starwars |> summarize(across(where(is.numeric), min_max))
## # A tibble: 1 × 6
##   height_min height_max mass_min mass_max birth_year_min birth_year_max
##        <int>      <int>    <dbl>    <dbl>          <dbl>          <dbl>
## 1         66        264       15     1358              8            896

Order matters. If you compute n = n() first, n is numeric, so a later across(where(is.numeric), sd) will try to take the SD of that constant and give NA. Put n = n() last.

across() replaces the old *_if(), *_at(), and *_all helpers. select() and rename() already use tidyselect; use rename_with() to change names with a function.

5. across() vs c_across()

The names are close, the jobs are not.

  • across() applies a function to each selected column separately.
  • c_across() combines the selected values within the current row.
xyz <- tibble(name = c("A", "B", "C"), x = c(10, 20, 30), y = c(20, 40, 60), z = c(30, 60, 90))

One mean per column (mean(x), mean(y), mean(z)):

xyz |> summarize(across(x:z, mean))
## # A tibble: 1 × 3
##       x     y     z
##   <dbl> <dbl> <dbl>
## 1    20    40    60
xyz |> mutate(across(x:z, ~ .x * 2))
## # A tibble: 3 × 4
##   name      x     y     z
##   <chr> <dbl> <dbl> <dbl>
## 1 A        20    40    60
## 2 B        40    80   120
## 3 C        60   120   180

One mean per row (mean(c(10, 20, 30)), mean(c(20, 40, 60)), …):

xyz |>
  rowwise() |>
  mutate(row_mean = mean(c_across(x:z))) |>
  ungroup()
## # A tibble: 3 × 5
##   name      x     y     z row_mean
##   <chr> <dbl> <dbl> <dbl>    <dbl>
## 1 A        10    20    30       20
## 2 B        20    40    60       40
## 3 C        30    60    90       60

A picture to remember:

          x     y     z
        ----------------
Row 1    10    20    30   ->  c_across()
Row 2    20    40    60   ->  c_across()
Row 3    30    60    90   ->  c_across()
         |     |     |
         v     v     v
            across()

across() goes down the columns. c_across() goes along a row. The c is for combine, as in c().

6. When you do not need rowwise()

Using several columns does not by itself call for rowwise(). These are all vectorized:

xyz |> mutate(total = x + y + z, difference = x - y, ratio = x / y, bigger = x > y)
## # A tibble: 3 × 8
##   name      x     y     z total difference ratio bigger
##   <chr> <dbl> <dbl> <dbl> <dbl>      <dbl> <dbl> <lgl> 
## 1 A        10    20    30    60        -10   0.5 FALSE 
## 2 B        20    40    60   120        -20   0.5 FALSE 
## 3 C        30    60    90   180        -30   0.5 FALSE

rowwise() is for functions like mean(), max(), min(), sd() that take a vector and return one value, when you want that value for each row.

So do not ask “Am I using several columns?” Ask:

  • Same function on each column separately? Use across().
  • Combine values from several columns within each row? Use rowwise() with c_across() (or rowSums() / rowMeans() for sums and means).
